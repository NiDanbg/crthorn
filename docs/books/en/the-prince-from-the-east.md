### Chapter 1

He wasn't ready to trust anyone yet, and the darkness no longer frightened him. He'd grown accustomed to it. The shrubs and their blurred gray silhouettes in the dark didn't wound him as they once had. He'd learned. Learned to trace even the faintest animal trails—when he stumbled upon those made by human feet, they might as well have been highways. He avoided the rutted wagon roads and the rare sensation of human presence. He walked without purpose but followed one direction: away from his brother. Betrayed or abandoned—what difference did it make when the result was the same? He just needed to put distance between them and not let himself be caught by the hounds his elder brother would send after him.

He crossed several mountain streams. Even swam nearly a mile along a river whose stony bed carried him easily through a gorge that had become impassable on foot. The current was swift but not enough to threaten him. Choices were scarce. He had to cover his tracks. He emerged from the foaming waters just before they plunged with a deafening roar into a waterfall's abyss, its base lined with jagged rocks. His wiry frame scaled the cliff splitting the torrent in two without difficulty, and from its crest, he leaped nearly six feet, tumbling onto the soft moss beneath the ferns of the left bank. The right side—farther from pursuit. He didn't know if they were coming, but he was certain they would be. His brother wouldn't let him go. Not now. Not after he'd questioned his leadership and their father's legacy.

Dawn was breaking. The moonlit grays of night yielded to the hues of day, urging him to hurry—to find shelter before daylight exposed him. 

Scanning the cliffs ahead for an overhang or crevice, his gaze snagged on the faint outline of a small cave entrance, barely darker than the rock, perched high on a sheer ridge. Safety. Without hesitation, his fingers sought cracks and ridges to climb the near-vertical face. Midway up, his foot landed on a level path carved into the cliffside—wide enough for a cart yet invisible from the river below. He paused. Considered taking it. Dismissed the thought as quickly as it came. The sun had cleared the peaks. He felt his vulnerability. Daylight travel still wasn't safe. He needed to hide. Rest.

The cave was shallow. Just a few paces of level stone floor before walls and ceiling narrowed inward. On the ground lay a woven nest of branches, long abandoned—some shattered bones suggesting its former owner had been large, predatory. He sank beside it, exhausted, and closed his eyes. Another nightmare-ridden sleep awaited him. And the voice—always the voice—pleading, whispering.


### Chapter 2

"I told you this dog is too old. It won't last." Noel, former caravan guard, veteran of countless battles and current aide to the General, was accustomed to having his words heard. The final years of his distinguished military career had changed him. His bearing had acquired the dignified air of a seasoned warrior so typical of veterans, while his temperament had smoothed and settled. Dozens of battles and the responsibilities heaped upon him had completed his transformation into a man worthy of serving the General. Yet glimmers of roguishness still surfaced in his gaze, and his shrewdness was legendary.

His words seemed to fall on deaf ears. The man they'd clearly been addressed to showed no reaction. Seated ramrod straight with an almost imperceptible forward lean, he gripped his horse's reins in his left hand while his stormy eyes remained fixed on the massive dog crouched thirty paces ahead. His blue eyes flashed beneath the shock of hair that had slipped free from the leather tie restraining his warrior's tail, the long strands falling across his azure cloak. The hilt of a longsword jutted above his left shoulder, while another hung at his right hip. A battle-hardened hand rested casually atop it, calloused by years of warfare. With subtle pressure from his thighs, he urged his mount forward to stand slightly apart from the following riders, never breaking his scrutinizing gaze from the dog.

There at the bend in the mountain path ahead, seated on its haunches with perked ears, the massive mastiff bearing the deceptively sweet name "Roschko" meticulously sampled the air.

Roschko had ranged ahead of the column but remained visibly close. The narrow rock-hewn path offered no room to stray, and the roaring river below drowned nearly all sound. Yet the dog's nose caught countless scents—both from tracks pressed into stone and those carried on mountain currents painting invisible portraits of hidden life.

A minute earlier, pursuing the sweet musk of mountain goats, Roschko had reached the bend when suddenly he froze. Overpowering aromas cascaded from the cliffs—an uncharacteristic spice blend laced with the startlingly familiar tang of human presence. Distant memories stirred, triggered by that human element in the scent, making him sit upright. His tail wagged briefly, stirring dust before hesitation took hold. Subtle differences emerged. Tiny, but definite. Disappointed, the mastiff shook his massive head, banishing memories, and sniffed again, exposing his muzzle to every air current. He wasn't mistaken. The scent remained, differences and all, but no danger registered. Turning, he looked back toward the approaching riders. He had sensed The Strong One watching from horseback, had noticed the tail's cautious motion. Good. Warning received. No danger! Duty satisfied, the mastiff rose confidently on four massive paws and slow-marched past the memory's source. The Friend would return someday!

"Old or not..." Reaching the dog's position, the scowling rider at the column's head raised a clenched fist, halting the riders behind. He surveyed the area meticulously. Though he noted the cave mouth's outline above, his gaze slid past it deliberately. For added effect, he even glanced downward at the raging river. He trusted the dog. The beast's instincts had saved him dozens of times. Roschko made it abundantly clear that whatever waited above posed no threat, but he'd privately noted their road wasn't entirely empty. "...Roschko stays with me."

Noel shook his head thoughtfully but found no words to contradict his general. Privately, he believed the general's shaggy terror would only hinder their journey. Standing beside the general's mount, he risked leaning closer.

"Your dog, your call, Thrower." Hearing his old nickname, the General's expression softened. Forehead creases eased as his lips curved slightly. He'd long noticed Noel only used that name when conceding defeat. His aide never grew accustomed to the mastiff despite years together.

"He's not mine, Noel. Belongs to Nick. I just... borrow his friendship. Until Nick returns."

"You still haven't accepted we'll never see him again?" Noel marveled that Thrower still expected his friend's return.

"Eleven years, Noel. Remember—for him it's barely... what? Thirteen months? Fourteen?"

"Even so—"

"I'll give it a while longer before giving up. Hope he's with Kira. That they're happy."

"But she died ages ago! I don't follow your meaning!" Noel never fully grasped the story of the Endless Ones, despite the general's nostalgic explanations.

"Oh, Noel." The General sighed wearily. "Weren't listening. She died Here, so she's There. Like Mira says: 'You were a dumb guard and dumb you'll stay.'"

Noel shuddered but mustered a strained smile. 

"Ugh! Don't mention that witch. She gives me chills." He paused for effect. "When did you last see your beloved Shade?"

Thrower laughed—a rare sound shedding his general's bearing, revealing the carefree guard of old.

"Just recently. Three days back." Noel stared, startled.

"She visited? At the inn? I noticed nothing!"

"Course not. She's a shade! Want trumpets announcing her? Maybe fanfare? Ha! You understand little, aide." With that, Thrower vanished again behind his general's mask.

"Yes, my General." Noel recognized the window to the past had closed. "Forward!" He signaled the riders, and the column resumed its slow advance toward Roschko disappearing around the next bend.
